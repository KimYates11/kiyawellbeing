The Turtle Theory ~ Are you moving at your own pace?

Why slowing down doesn’t mean you’re falling behind.

K
Kim Yates
Founder, KIYA Wellbeing

“Nature does not hurry, yet everything is accomplished.”

— Traditionally attributed to Lao Tzu

Have you ever been feeling pretty good about where you’re at in life… until you catch up with a friend, scroll through social media or hear about someone else’s latest achievement?

They’ve bought a house, landed their dream job, started a business, found love or booked another amazing holiday.

And you’re genuinely happy for them.

But then that little voice creeps in…

Am I doing enough?
Should I be further along by now?
Why does everyone else seem to have it all figured out?

Sound familiar?

I recently came across something called The Turtle Theory, and it really resonated with me.

It’s a beautiful reminder that we all have our own journey, our own pace and our own timing.

And perhaps we don’t need to be in such a hurry to get somewhere all the time.

The Turtle Theory ~ One stroke at a time

Imagine a turtle swimming through the ocean towards the shore.

Some days the water is calm, the current is gentle and moving forward feels effortless.

Other days the ocean is choppy, the current is strong and the turtle has to work that little bit harder.

But it keeps swimming.

One stroke at a time.

It doesn’t look at another turtle swimming past and think, Why am I not there yet?

It doesn’t question whether it’s doing enough or whether it should be moving faster.

It simply keeps going, adjusting to the conditions around it.

And isn’t that such a lovely metaphor for life?

We all experience different seasons, challenges, opportunities and setbacks.

Sometimes everything seems to flow.

Other times life asks us to slow down, change direction, rest or simply ride the waves.

Just because someone else appears to be moving faster doesn’t mean you’re falling behind.

The pressure to always be doing more

I think so many of us have become conditioned to believe that we should constantly be achieving something.

The next goal. The next milestone. The next thing on the to-do list.

And when we finally get there?

We’re often already thinking about what’s next.

I know I’ve been guilty of this myself.

Having worked in the corporate world for many years, experiencing burnout and eventually making the decision to follow my passion for wellbeing, I’ve learnt that life doesn’t always unfold according to the timeline we imagine.

Building a business, changing careers, navigating relationships and creating a life that feels aligned all take time.

And even when we’re doing something we love, it can be easy to fall into the trap of thinking we should be doing more.

But I’ve also learnt that being busy doesn’t necessarily mean we’re moving in the right direction.

Sometimes we become so focused on where we’re going that we forget to appreciate where we are.

Which brings me to one of my favourite teachings from yoga philosophy.

Santosha ~ The art of contentment

In yoga, Santosha is one of the five Niyamas, or personal observances, outlined in Patanjali’s Yoga Sutras.

Santosha translates to contentment.

It’s the practice of finding peace and appreciation in the present moment, rather than constantly looking outside ourselves for fulfilment.

And I think this is something we could all benefit from practising a little more.

Santosha isn’t about giving up on your goals, settling for less or not wanting to grow.

It’s about learning to be content with where you are while still working towards where you want to be.

We can be grateful for what we have and still have dreams.

We can celebrate someone else’s achievements without questioning our own.

We can be ambitious without constantly putting pressure on ourselves to achieve more.

And perhaps most importantly, we can recognise that our worth isn’t measured by our productivity, success or the milestones we’ve reached.

You don’t have to wait until everything falls into place to feel content with your life.

What yoga, breathwork and meditation have taught me about slowing down

One of the biggest lessons I’ve learnt through more than 20 years of personal practice, and now teaching yoga, meditation and breathwork, is the importance of creating space.

Space to pause.
Space to breathe.
Space to simply be.

We’re so often living in our heads, replaying the past or worrying about the future, that we forget to connect with what’s happening right now.

And this is where our practices can be such beautiful tools.

Through yoga, we learn to meet ourselves where we are.

Some days we feel strong, energised and balanced. Other days we’re tired, distracted or less flexible.

Our practice reminds us that we don’t need to force, push or compare. We can simply listen to our bodies and work with what we have on any given day.

Through breathwork, we can create a pause between our thoughts and our reactions.

When we feel overwhelmed or caught up in comparison, taking a few slow, conscious breaths can help settle our nervous system and bring us back into the present moment.

Through meditation, we practise observing our thoughts without becoming consumed by them.

We begin to notice that I’m not doing enough or I should be further along is just a thought.

Not necessarily the truth.

We don’t have to believe every thought that passes through our mind.

And I think that’s such an empowering realisation.

Sometimes all we need is a few moments of stillness to change our perspective.

Three gentle reminders when you feel like you’re falling behind

1. Pause and take a breath

The next time you catch yourself comparing your life to someone else’s, pause.

Before you start questioning your choices or adding another ten things to your to-do list, come back to your breath.

Try this simple breathing practice:

  • Inhale gently through your nose for a count of four.
  • Exhale slowly through your nose for a count of six.
  • Repeat for five rounds, keeping the breath soft and comfortable.

As you breathe, notice whether your shoulders soften, your thoughts settle or you feel a little more present.

Then ask yourself:

Am I actually unhappy with where I am, or have I just started comparing myself to someone else?

It’s a simple question, but sometimes it changes everything.

2. Celebrate how far you’ve come

We’re often so focused on what’s ahead that we forget to acknowledge everything we’ve already achieved, overcome and learnt.

Think about where you were this time last year.

Maybe you’ve navigated a difficult period.
Maybe you’ve started something new.
Maybe you’ve learnt to set boundaries, walked away from something that wasn’t right for you or simply made more time for yourself.

These are all achievements worth celebrating.

I often remind my students that yoga isn’t about how deep you can get into a pose or whether you can do what the person beside you is doing.

It’s about how you feel, what you learn and the connection you create with yourself along the way.

And life is really no different.

Progress isn’t always something we can see from the outside.

3. Take one small stroke forward

Like the turtle, we don’t have to reach the shore in one big effort.

Sometimes one small step is enough.

Maybe it’s sending that email you’ve been putting off.
Booking the yoga class.
Spending ten minutes meditating.
Going for a walk in nature.

Or perhaps it’s recognising that what you actually need today is rest.

We don’t need to constantly push ourselves forward to prove we’re making progress.

Small, intentional steps add up.

And sometimes slowing down gives us the clarity to see where we actually want to go.

A little meditation ~ Trusting your own timing

If this is something you’ve been feeling lately, take a few minutes to reconnect with yourself.

Find a comfortable seated position or lie down.
Close your eyes if that feels comfortable.

Take a slow breath in through your nose.
And gently exhale.

Allow your shoulders to soften.
Relax your jaw.
Feel the support of the earth beneath you.

With each inhale, imagine creating a little more space.

With each exhale, imagine letting go of the pressure to be somewhere other than where you are.

Silently repeat:

I am exactly where I am.
I honour how far I’ve come.
I release the need to compare.
I trust my own pace.

Stay here for a few more breaths.

Nothing to achieve.
Nowhere else you need to be.
Just this moment.

A final thought ~ Trust the journey

Perhaps one of the greatest lessons we can learn is to stop trying to control every part of our journey and instead begin to trust the process.

Trust that not everything needs to happen right now.

Trust that the challenges, changes and unexpected detours can teach us something.

Trust that there is wisdom in slowing down, just as there is growth in moving forward.

We spend so much of our lives striving to become the next version of ourselves that we sometimes forget to appreciate who we are today.

And perhaps that’s where the real practice lies.

Not in doing more.
Not in getting somewhere faster.

But in learning to surrender a little, listen to our intuition and reconnect with the rhythm of our own lives.

“Perhaps the greatest act of trust is to stop rushing towards the person you think you should be, and honour the person you are becoming.”

Nature doesn’t rush.
The seasons change when they’re ready.
Flowers bloom in their own time.

And the turtle keeps swimming, one stroke at a time.

Perhaps we could all take a little inspiration from that.

Life isn’t a competition, and we’re not all following the same path.

There will be seasons of growth, seasons of change and seasons where we’re simply learning to be.

And all of them have value.

So the next time you feel that familiar pressure to do more, be more or get somewhere faster, take a breath.

Come back to yourself.

Remember how far you’ve already come.

And trust that you can continue moving forward without rushing through the life you’re living right now.

Your journey. Your pace. Your timing.

A little reflection for the week

Take a moment with your morning tea, after a yoga class or during a few minutes of quiet meditation, and ask yourself:

  • Where in my life am I putting unnecessary pressure on myself?
  • What have I achieved or overcome that I haven’t given myself enough credit for?
  • What would change if I trusted my own timing a little more?

Perhaps write your thoughts in a journal, or simply sit with whatever comes up.

And remember…

You can be a work in progress and still be proud of where you are today.

With kindness,
